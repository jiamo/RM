# M2-BRUN-TWIN — Brun's twin-prime bound from the squarefree large sieve

## Task

Draw the two-class consequence of Montgomery's sieve of `M2-LARGE-SIEVE-SQUAREFREE`. The rows
`M2-BRUN-TITCHMARSH` and its progression form sift by one residue class modulo each prime, and
their sifting sum is of size `log Q`. Sifting the twin primes removes *two* classes modulo every
odd prime, the sifting sum becomes of size `(log Q)²`, and the sieve gives the upper bound of
Brun's theorem for the twin primes.

## The statement

For every `N` with `log N ≥ 32`,

```
#{ n < N : n and n + 2 both prime } ≤ 900 N / (log N)² + √N + 1.
```

## The proof

**The sieve.** `card_le_sifted_squarefree` of `M2-LARGE-SIEVE-SQUAREFREE` is instantiated at the
weights `w 2 = 1` and `w p = p − 2` for odd `p`. A twin prime `n > Q` satisfies `n % p ≠ 0` (else
`p ∣ n` with `n` prime and `n > p`) and `n % p ≠ p − 2` (else `p ∣ n + 2`, again a prime exceeding
`p`), so the image of the sifted set modulo an odd prime `p ≤ Q` has at most `p − 2` elements;
modulo `2` it is `{1}`. Montgomery's inequality gives `|A| · twinSiftSum Q ≤ Q² + π N`.

**The sifting sum.** The point of the row is the lower bound `twinSiftSum Q ≫ (log Q)²`, proved
in three steps.

1. *The odd weights dominate the odd harmonic sum.* For odd squarefree `d`, the fibre bound
   `sum_inv_primeFactors_eq_le` of `M2-BRUN-TITCHMARSH` gives
   `∑_{m ≤ X, rad m = d} 1/m ≤ ∏_{p ∣ d} 1/(p − 1) = sieveWeight d`, and every odd `m ≤ X` lies in
   exactly one such fibre, whose index is odd. Splitting the harmonic sum into its even part
   `(1/2) ∑_{k ≤ X/2} 1/k` and its odd part yields
   `oddSieveTotal X ≥ ∑_{m ≤ X odd} 1/m ≥ (log X)/2 − 1/2`.

2. *Coprimality costs a factor `9/16`.* A non-coprime pair of odd squarefree numbers has a common
   odd prime factor `p ≤ X`, and the odd squarefree `d ≤ X` divisible by `p` carry at most
   `(1/(p − 1)) oddSieveTotal X` — the map `d ↦ d/p` is injective and divides the weight by
   `p − 1`. A union bound over `p` and `∑_{p ≥ 3} 1/(p − 1)² ≤ (1/4) ∑_{j ≥ 1} 1/j² ≤ 7/16` (the
   `p − 1` are distinct even numbers) bound the non-coprime pairs by `(7/16) oddSieveTotal X²`.

3. *The pairs are a lower bound for the twin sifting sum.* Grouping the coprime pairs `(a, b)` of
   odd squarefree numbers up to `X` by their product `q = a b ≤ X² ≤ Q`, each pair in the fibre
   over `q` contributes `sieveWeight a · sieveWeight b = sieveWeight q` (multiplicativity), and
   the fibre has at most `2^{ω(q)}` elements, since a squarefree `a` is determined by its set of
   prime factors, a subset of those of `q`. Hence the fibre sum is at most
   `2^{ω(q)} ∏_{p ∣ q} 1/(p − 1) = ∏_{p ∣ q} 2/(p − 1) ≤ ∏_{p ∣ q} 2/(p − 2)`, the twin factor of
   `q` — the last inequality being where the *two* sifted classes are used.

**Assembling.** With `Q = ⌊√N⌋` and `X = ⌊√Q⌋`, `half_log_sub_log_two_le_log_sqrt` applied twice
gives `log X ≥ (log N)/4 − (3/2) log 2`, so `twinSiftSum Q ≥ (9/16)((log X)/2 − 1/2)²` is at least
`(486/100000)(log N)²` once `log N ≥ 32`. The sieve bounds the twin primes above `Q` by
`(Q² + π N)/twinSiftSum Q ≤ 4.15 N/((486/100000)(log N)²) ≤ 900 N/(log N)²`, and the twin primes
below `Q` are at most `Q + 1 ≤ √N + 1`.

## What is *not* proved

* The constant is not sharp. The sharp sieve gives `(8 + o(1)) C₂ N/(log N)²` with `C₂` the twin
  prime constant; `900` carries the factor `π` of this repository's large sieve in place of `1`,
  the rigid level `Q = ⌊√N⌋` (which halves the logarithm, hence quarters its square), the
  estimate `9/16` for the coprimality loss, and `∏_{p ∣ q} 2/(p − 1)` in place of the singular
  series. The threshold `log N ≥ 32` is an artefact of the same additive losses.
* No *lower* bound for the number of twin primes is proved; that is the twin-prime conjecture,
  and it is open.
* No numerical value or digit of Brun's constant is certified here. (The *convergence* of
  `∑ 1/p` over the twin primes, Brun's theorem, is drawn from this bound in `M2-BRUN-CONSTANT`.)
* Nothing about `ζ` follows: no zero-free region, no zero-density estimate, no mean value
  theorem.
