# Koksma's inequality

Date: 2026-09-06
Module: `RiemannHypothesis/Koksma.lean`

## Why

`M1-DISCREPANCY` defines the discrepancy

    D_N(x) = sup { |#{n < N : ⟨xₙ⟩ ∈ [a, b)}/N − (b − a)| : 0 ≤ a ≤ b ≤ 1 }

and `M1-DISCREPANCY-CRITERION` shows that its vanishing is exactly equidistribution.  What is
still missing is the reason the discrepancy is *the* quantity to bound: it controls the error of
numerical integration along the sequence.  That is Koksma's inequality, and it converts every
discrepancy bound of the repository into an explicit error bound for an average.

## What is proved

* `sum_mul_sub_succ` — summation by parts on `range K`, with the boundary terms `a 0 · S 0` and
  `a K · S K` written out.
* `abs_tailDev_le_discrepancy` — the deviation of a tail interval `[j/K, 1)` is at most `D_N`.
* `sum_stepLeft_le_sum_fract`, `sum_fract_le_sum_stepRight` — monotonicity brackets the sum of
  the values between the two step sums built from the endpoints of the cells.
* `abs_sum_cellDev_le`, `abs_sum_cellDev_right_le` — each cell deviation sum satisfies
  `|∑_j w_j (c_j/N − 1/K)| ≤ (f 1 − f 0) D_N(x)`, with `w_j` the value of `f` at either endpoint
  of the `j`-th cell.
* `riemannSum_le_integral_le`, `riemannSum_right_sub_left` — the left and right Riemann sums of a
  monotone `f` bracket `∫₀¹ f` and differ by `(f 1 − f 0)/K`.
* `koksma_monotone` — **Koksma's inequality**: for a nondecreasing `f` on `[0, 1]` — no
  continuity assumed — every sequence `x` and every `N ≥ 1`,

      |(1/N) ∑_{n<N} f(⟨xₙ⟩) − ∫₀¹ f| ≤ (f 1 − f 0) · D_N(x) .

* `koksma_sub_monotone` — the same bound for a difference `f = g - h` of two nondecreasing
  functions, with `(g 1 - g 0) + (h 1 - h 0)` in place of `f 1 - f 0`.
* `koksma_lipschitz` — for an `L`-Lipschitz `f` the same error is at most `3 L · D_N(x)`.
* `tendsto_average_of_tendsto_discrepancy`, `tendsto_average_quadratic` — the limiting forms; the
  second is the concrete instance `xₙ = α n²` with `α` irrational.

## Proof

Cut `[0, 1)` into the `K` cells `[j/K, (j+1)/K)`, and let `c_j` be the number of `n < N` whose
fractional part lies in the `j`-th cell.

1. *The sandwich.*  Because `f` is nondecreasing, replacing each value `f(⟨xₙ⟩)` by the value at
   the left endpoint of its cell decreases the sum, and by the value at the right endpoint
   increases it.  So the average lies between the two step averages `∑_j (c_j/N) f(j/K)` and
   `∑_j (c_j/N) f((j+1)/K)`.  For the same reason `∫₀¹ f` lies between the left and the right
   Riemann sums, which differ by exactly `(f 1 − f 0)/K`.
2. *The cell deviation sums.*  Each step average differs from the corresponding Riemann sum by
   `∑_j w_j (c_j/N − 1/K)`.  Writing `S_j` for the deviation of the tail interval `[j/K, 1)`, one
   has `c_j/N − 1/K = S_j − S_{j+1}`, and summation by parts turns the sum into a sum of
   consecutive differences of `f` weighted by the `S_j`.  The boundary terms vanish, because
   `S_0 = 0` (every fractional part lies in `[0, 1)`) and `S_K = 0` (the interval `[1, 1)` is
   empty).  Since `f` is nondecreasing the differences are nonnegative and telescope to at most
   `f 1 − f 0`, while `|S_j| ≤ D_N` by `le_discrepancy`.

Combining the two, the error is at most `(f 1 − f 0) D_N + (f 1 − f 0)/K` for every `K ≥ 1`, and
letting `K` grow gives the inequality.

The bounded-variation form follows by additivity, and the Lipschitz form by writing an
`L`-Lipschitz `f` as the difference of the nondecreasing functions `t ↦ f t + L t` and `t ↦ L t`,
of total rises `f 1 − f 0 + L ≤ 2L` and `L`.

## Scope

The integrand is presented either as a monotone function or as an explicit difference of two
monotone functions.  No total-variation functional is introduced, so the statement is not phrased
for a general function of bounded variation, and the constant is the sum of the rises of the
given decomposition rather than the total variation.  The constant `3 L` of the Lipschitz form is
not sharp — the sharp value is `L`.  Nothing here concerns `ζ`.

## Adversarial revalidation: M1-KOKSMA

* **Is the inequality vacuous, or trivially true?**  No.  Both sides are finite and the right
  side is genuinely small for the sequences of `M1-DISCREPANCY`: for a badly approximable `α`
  the discrepancy of `α n` is `O(N^(−1/9))`, so the integration error inherits that rate.  For a
  non-equidistributed sequence the bound is not vacuous either — `D_N ≤ 1` always, and
  `half_le_discrepancy_const_zero` shows the constant sequence really has `D_N ≥ 1/2`.
* **Is the discretisation step hiding the difficulty?**  No.  The discretisation is exact: the
  two step sums bracket the average, with no error term at all, and it is the summation by parts,
  not the discretisation, that produces the factor `D_N`; the constant in front of `D_N` is
  exactly the rise `f 1 − f 0` and is not degraded by the limit in `K`.
* **Does the boundary term of the summation by parts do the work?**  No, it vanishes:
  `S_0 = 0` because every fractional part lies in `[0, 1)`, and `S_K = 0` because the interval
  `[1, 1)` is empty.  Had it not vanished, the bound would have carried a term `|f 0| D_N`,
  which is not of the stated shape.
* **Is the Lipschitz form stronger than the monotone one?**  No, it is deduced from it, with a
  constant `3 L` that the deduction costs; the monotone statement is the substance.
* **Anything claimed about `ζ`?**  Nothing.  These are sequences modulo one, not zeta phases.
